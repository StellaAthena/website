My research sits at the intersection of machine learning, natural language processing, and the philosophy of science. I am broadly interested in understanding what language models learn, why they behave the way they do, and how we can build better tools for studying them.

## Large Language Models

I study the behavior, capabilities, and limitations of large language models. This includes work on training dynamics, emergent abilities, in-context learning, and understanding how scale affects model behavior. I am particularly interested in what these models actually "know" and how we can rigorously evaluate their knowledge and reasoning abilities.

## AI Interpretability

I am interested in mechanistic interpretability—understanding the internal computations of neural networks at a detailed, circuit-level. I work on developing tools and techniques to understand what representations models learn and how those representations give rise to model behavior.

## Evaluation and Benchmarking

Rigorous evaluation is essential for scientific progress in AI. I work on developing better evaluation methodologies, identifying limitations of existing benchmarks, and understanding what our metrics actually measure. I am particularly skeptical of claims based on poorly-designed evaluations.

## Open-Source AI and Reproducibility

I am a strong advocate for open-source AI research. I believe that scientific progress requires reproducibility, and that open models and open data are essential for the broader research community to build on each other's work. Much of my work involves releasing models, datasets, and code openly.

## Societal Impacts of AI

I think carefully about the societal implications of AI systems, including questions of fairness, bias, misuse, and the appropriate deployment of AI in high-stakes settings.
